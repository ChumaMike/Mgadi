# Mgadi payment-diversion playbook: DRAFT for practitioner review

The app shows exactly these steps, one per screen. The AI never writes or changes them; it only picks which playbook to open.

## Who this is for
The owner or bookkeeper of a South African small business (5 to 50 staff, no IT person) who has just found out that a supplier payment went to an account that a criminal supplied.

## Step 1: Call your bank's fraud line now.
- Why: the bank is the only one who can try to stop or recall the payment. Minutes count.
- Pick your bank and call the official fraud line (from our dated directory, or the back of your card). Never a number from the email.
- Say: "I made a payment to a fraudulent account. Please try to stop or recall it."
- Have ready: the date, the amount and the account it went to.
- Write down the reference number.

## Step 2: Confirm with your supplier on a number you already had.
- Why: don't reply to the email or call a number in it; the criminal may be reading that mailbox.
- Phone the supplier on a number from an old invoice, your contacts or their website. Ask: "Did you change your banking details?"
- Tell them their email may also be hacked.

## Step 3: Check your mailbox for hidden rules and strange sign-ins.
- Why: criminals add hidden forwarding or inbox rules to watch for payments.
- Use a different, clean device if you can.
- Delete forwarding and inbox rules you did not create; check recent sign-ins; change the password; turn on two-step verification.
- Microsoft 365: Settings > Mail > Rules; Settings > Mail > Forwarding; mysignins.microsoft.com.
- Gmail: Settings > See all settings > Filters and Blocked Addresses; Forwarding and POP/IMAP; myaccount.google.com/security.

## Step 4: Keep the evidence.
- Save the "new banking details" email and the proof of payment. Don't delete anything.
- Mgadi fingerprints each file (SHA-256) on the phone. Nothing is uploaded.

## Then: who else to tell (shown in the incident pack)
- **SAPS:** open a fraud case and keep the CAS number. Crime Stop 08600 10111.
- **Insurer:** if you have business or cyber cover, tell them today; ask whether social-engineering or funds-transfer loss is covered.
- **IT support:** if you have one, ask them to check the mailbox and devices.
- **Information Regulator (POPIA):** if the hacked mailbox held personal information about clients or staff, you *may* need to report a security compromise under section 22 via eservices.inforegulator.org.za. This is not legal advice.
